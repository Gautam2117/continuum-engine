## Why this is a new editorial revision

The original local Qwen draft claimed stopping Earth's rotation creates a 58-day solar day. Its critic suggested a different unsupported 47-day figure. Both are incorrect. The revised script independently derives an annual solar day **only under the explicit hypothetical** that Earth's axial spin stops relative to distant stars while its orbital revolution continues normally, and that the catastrophic forces of an actual instant stop are excluded. It does not equate lunar tidal locking with zero axial spin.

## Claim-by-claim source evidence

* Earth's mean orbit is about 365.25 days, its spin is about 23.9 hours, axial tilt 23.4 degrees and its diameter about 12,756 km: [NASA Earth Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/).
* The rotation-versus-revolution distinction, mean solar time and sidereal time: [NASA Spaceflight Reference Systems](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter2-1/).
* Once axial rotation is removed but orbit continues, the solar direction circles the fixed surface about once per year; for an idealized equatorial observer, ~half a year daylight and ~half darkness: [NASA GSFC Earth and Moon question 24](https://science.gsfc.nasa.gov/attic/cosmicopia.gsfc.nasa.gov/qa_earth.html).
* The Moon orbits in roughly 27 days. This does not determine the no-spin solar day: [NASA Moon Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/).
* Equatorial speed is estimated using `v = π × equatorial_diameter / rotation_period`: `π × 12,756 km / 23.9 h ≈ 1,677 km/h`; the displayed ~1,670 km/h is a rounded estimate. The actual equator is not an exact sphere and the sidereal period rounds this further.
